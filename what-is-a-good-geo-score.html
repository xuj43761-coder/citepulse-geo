Why Chasing 100/100 Is Usually the Wrong Move

Once a page crosses roughly 85/100, spending another hour pushing it from 88 to 99 rarely changes whether ChatGPT Search or Google AI Overviews picks it up. AI search systems rely heavily on traditional search retrieval and competitive topical depth. A 97/100 page targeting an ultra-competitive head term will still lose to stronger domains, while an 86/100 page answering a narrow, underserved buyer question can earn citations quickly.

What to Do Right After Your GEO Score Passes 80+

  1. Verify there is no single failing pillar: Make sure none of the five 20-point dimensions (Crawler Hygiene, JSON-LD Schema, Entity Clarity, Fact Density, FAQ Structure) sits below 12/20.
  2. Inspect who ranks on traditional SERPs for the exact question: Because AI search engines ground answers in top search results, check keyword difficulty and current SERP competitors using a lightweight keyword suite like Mangools (KWFinder & SERPChecker) instead of running another readiness scorer.
  3. Publish narrow "one question, one page" assets: Target specific comparison, pricing, and troubleshooting queries where incumbent pages are bloated or generic.
  4. Keep crawler access and root files clean: Confirm OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot are allowed in robots.txt and list your canonical answers in llms.txt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a 95+ GEO score guarantee ChatGPT or Perplexity will cite my page?

No. A GEO score measures on-page structural and technical readiness—clear entity definitions, verifiable numbers, crawler hygiene, visible FAQs, and valid JSON-LD. Actual citation share also depends on domain authority, crawl access, SERP competition, and external mentions.

What are the 97/100 and 12/100 scores inside CitePulse GEO?

They are deterministic built-in example templates included in our browser auditor so you can test how the rubric behaves: 97/100 represents a complete reference page with definitions, stats, FAQ, and JSON-LD, while 12/100 represents a thin page with almost only bare metadata.

Why can an 86/100 page still miss points?

Because the rubric weights five 20-point pillars independently. A well-written guide with strong definitions and statistics can score 86/100 overall while still dropping 10 to 14 points on visible FAQ coverage or matching FAQPage JSON-LD.
